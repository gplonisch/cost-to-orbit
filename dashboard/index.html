Cost-to-Orbit

Launch price per kilogram to low Earth orbit, with the price basis kept visible. Program costs, government contracts and commercial list prices are different numbers and are not averaged together here.

The frontier decline

Cheapest published price per kg in each anchor year. Log scale, because the claim being examined is a constant proportional decline. The 1981 spike is the Space Shuttle: reusability without cheap refurbishment made orbit more expensive for twenty-five years.

What you can actually buy today

Commercial prices only, so every bar is comparable. The frontier figure assumes you buy an entire rocket at its maximum expendable payload. A customer with a small satellite buys rideshare instead, at several times the rate.

Every row

The full cross-section, including the rows that are not comparable to each other. Sorted cheapest first. Unpriced vehicles appear last and are excluded from every statistic above.
